Start here

This page compares the same donors, year after year. A donor is counted only if every year from 2018 through 2026 has been checked in the state's filings, so a year with no money shows as $0, not as a gap. That keeps the comparison fair: the lines below change because the giving changed, not because we looked harder in some years.

The short version. Comparing the two full years at the start (2018–2019) with the two most recent full years (2024–2025), every industry on this page gave more:
  • Real estate about 1.7 times as much
  • Energy about 2 times as much
  • Tech about 2.2 times as much (but the companies giving changed)
  • Everything else about 3.5 times as much, mostly from two donors who gave little or nothing in 2018–2019
2026 is shown, but it runs only through the latest reports filed (mid-to-late September 2026), so it is not a full year.
Real estate is measured differently. Energy, tech and "everything else" are mostly companies writing checks, so their numbers are contributions given. The real estate numbers are what the Realtor and home builder PACs spent, counted once: RPAC's checks to candidates and campaigns, plus everything the local boards and builder PACs spent (which includes mailers, ads and, for the builder PACs, some of their own fundraising costs). RPAC's transfers down to local boards and up to the national Realtors PAC are left out so no dollar is counted twice. See What this doesn't show.

Elections every year — do the numbers show it?

Utah has an election every year, but not the same kind:

  • Even years (2018, 2020, 2022, 2024, 2026): the Legislature (all House seats, about half the Senate), county offices and Congress. Governor and other statewide offices are on the ballot in presidential years (2020, 2024, 2028).
  • Odd years (2019, 2021, 2023, 2025): city and town elections — mayors and councils.
  • The Legislature meets every year, January through early March.

Questions to ask — and whom to ask

  • Your legislator and your city council: Which of these industries have you received money from, and how do you decide when to step back from a vote that affects a donor?
  • The Utah Association of Realtors: How are the $50–$150 RPAC amounts billed to members, and how does a member opt out?
  • The Salt Lake Board of Realtors and Northern Wasatch Realtors: What did the National Association of Realtors grants pay for, and which local races did they target?
  • The Lieutenant Governor's Office (which runs disclosures.utah.gov): What does the "Other" purpose category cover, and should a payment filed that way require a description?
  • Your county clerk: In odd-year city elections, where can voters see the outside mailers paid for by PACs?
  • Lawmakers on the committees that write lobbying rules: Should a lobbyist's clients and the campaign checks from those same clients be shown together in one place?

What this doesn't show

  • Not every donor. Only donors with every year 2018–2026 checked are in the chart. Left out for that reason (their years aren't all checked yet): Torus (checked 2022–2026), Chevron (2022–2026), EnergySolutions (no year-by-year figures yet), Dominion Energy PAC, Utah Petroleum Association PAC (2020–2026), The Boeing Company PAC and The Home Depot PAC (2020–2026), Zions Bancorporation PAC (2021–2026), Utah Chamber PAC, Utah Tech Leads, Slopes PAC, Meta's Forge the Future Project (2026 only), and rPlus Energies (2026 only). Their full totals remain on each industry's own page.
  • Why the numbers differ from an earlier draft. An earlier working version of these comparisons included Torus in energy (its years before 2022 haven't been checked) and Google, Oracle, Amazon, Creekstone and Microsoft in tech (their year-by-year figures aren't in the Ledger's data files, so they could not be re-checked for this page). Both were removed for that reason. The comparison also now uses full years only (2018–19 vs. 2024–25).
  • Real estate is spending, not contributions. See the box at the top. It includes mailers, ads and, for the builder PACs, some of their own costs (for example, Utah Valley Home Builders PAC's largest recipient is its own association). RPAC's 2018 figures are from a full re-pull on Oct. 3, 2026; the Real Estate page still shows RPAC for 2019–2026.
  • Not federal money. Only Utah state filings. Federal PACs (for example, PacifiCorp's federal PAC) and federal races are not included.
  • Not lobbying spending. Lobbyist pay from clients is reported separately and is not added to any total here.
  • Not adjusted for inflation. Prices rose roughly 25–30% from 2018 to 2025, so part of any increase is inflation.
  • 2026 is partial. Most PACs' latest reports run through mid-to-late September 2026.
  • A pattern is not a promise. Giving more in a year does not show that any vote or decision was bought. This page reports what was filed.
