01Who Clyde Companies is

Clyde Companies, Inc. is based in Orem. Its own history page lists the companies it runs: W.W. Clyde (heavy civil construction), Sunroc, Sunpro, Geneva Rock Products, Beehive Insurance Agency, GWC Capital (land development, first called Clyde Properties) and Bridgesource. [2]

This page counts money reported from Clyde Companies and those companies only. It does not count money from Clyde family members giving as private people.

Staker Parson is not counted here. Some earlier Hive pages called Staker Parson Companies a Clyde subsidiary. That was wrong, and those pages now carry a dated correction. Staker Parson is owned by CRH; a 2026 job posting describes it as "Staker Parson Companies, a CRH company." [3] Engineering News-Record reported in 2015 that Oldcastle, its earlier parent, bought Staker in 1995. [4] For reference, the same data shows $200,150 in 124 contributions from Staker Parson, kept separate from every Clyde figure on this page. [1]

08Clyde's land and water records

Nordic Village PID (Ogden Valley). Clyde Capital Group holds 45% of Nordic Village Venture LLC, the developer entity, according to the project's Master Development Agreement beneficiary document. Those land records are covered in the Hive's Nordic Village case file and in Follow the Deed. [6][7]

A Washington County water right. GWC Capital, LLC filed change application a54441 on April 7, 2026, covering 1,314.083 acre-feet near St. George. Nobody protested, and the State Engineer approved it on Sept. 18, 2026. [8] The filing says part of the original right has been used to wash gravel by "Western Rock Products, Inc." Utah's business registry lists Western Rock Products as an assumed name owned by Staker & Parson Companies, which is CRH-owned. [8][9] The right's own records show approved non-use filings in 2008, 2013 and 2020, before GWC acquired it by an addendum to deed dated Feb. 1, 2023. [8]

09Questions to ask, and whom to ask

Your legislator or the governor's office: did they vote on or sign bills affecting road building, gravel, concrete or land development in the years they received Clyde money? The Hive's Bill-Tracker lists many of those bills.

The Lieutenant Governor's elections office: why do Clyde Companies' own corporate reports appear for only 2018, 2019 and 2021 when its contributions appear on recipients' reports every year?

The Utah Division of Water Rights: where can the public read the State Engineer's Sept. 18, 2026, order on application a54441?

11How we built this

We downloaded the state's yearly data files from disclosures.utah.gov for candidates, PACs, political parties and corporations, 2018 through 2026, on Oct. 19, 2026. We kept every line where the contributor name is Clyde Companies or one of the companies on its own history page, in every spelling filed (for example "Clyde Companies Inc.", "CLYDE COMPANIES INC" and "Sunroc Coproration"). Totals count only contributions on filed reports. Our count matched the state files line for line: 399 lines in all, before we set aside Staker Parson, purchases, Clydeco and unfiled reports. [1]
