Who these PACs are

Every leadership and industry PAC that appears as a recipient on this page — what it is and who runs it, per each PAC's own official state filing.

UHRECUtah House Republican Election Committee
The Utah House Republican caucus's official leadership PAC. Per its Statement of Organization: Mike Schultz (Speaker of the House) and Casey Snider (Majority Leader) are Primary Officers; Candice Pierucci (Majority Whip) is an Officer. Kyle Palmer is CFO/Treasurer, with sole signature authority over disbursements.
URSCCUtah Republican Senate Campaign Committee
The Utah Senate Republican caucus's official leadership PAC. Per its Statement of Organization: J. Stuart Adams (Senate President) and Kirk Cullimore (State Senator) are Primary Officers. Kyle Palmer is CFO/Treasurer — the same treasurer who runs UHREC.
Adams Leadership PAC
Senate President J. Stuart Adams' personal leadership PAC — separate from URSCC, the caucus-wide committee he also co-leads. Filed from Lehi, UT.
House Speaker Leadership PAC
Speaker Mike Schultz's personal leadership PAC — separate from UHREC, the caucus-wide committee he also co-leads. Director: Mike Schultz; Chief of Staff: Abby Osborne; CFO/Treasurer: Kyle Palmer.
Governors Leadership PAC
A gubernatorial leadership PAC, active in this dataset in 2018 and 2020. Current officer/leadership structure not yet independently confirmed in this research.
Dominion Energy PAC
Per its Statement of Organization (created 4/11/2017): Chairman Corynne Arnett (Dominion SVP, Regulatory Affairs & Customer Experience), Vice Chairman Darius Johnson, Treasurer Nathan Frost. Affiliated with and funded by Dominion Energy Inc. Its own reporting years run 2017-2024; it filed a Statement of Dissolution on 9/27/2024.
Utah Petroleum Association PAC
Chairman Brad Shafer (Marathon Petroleum); Vice Chairman Chad Calvert (Chevron); Secretary/Treasurer Rikki Hrenko-Browning (President, Utah Petroleum Association); Trustees Cameron Cuch and Spencer Kimball. Funded largely by the trade association itself and member oil companies.
Utah County Legislative PACUCL PAC
Created 3/9/2025 at the same Layton address as UHREC and URSCC. Per its Statement of Organization: President Stephanie Gricius (the sitting HD-50 representative) and Treasurer Kyle Palmer.
Worth noting: Kyle Palmer serves as CFO/Treasurer of four separate PACs appearing on this page at once — UHREC, URSCC, Adams Leadership PAC, and House Speaker Leadership PAC — giving him disbursement authority across every major Republican legislative leadership committee in the state simultaneously. This is his registered role per each PAC's own official filing, not an inference.
Why totals on this page are hard to fully trace: most donors here give through more than one channel at once — a direct check to an individual legislator, plus separate checks to one or more of these leadership PACs, which then redistribute their own pooled money to many of the same legislators. A single donor's real influence on a specific recipient can arrive by both paths simultaneously, and those paths don't always show up connected in a single disclosures.utah.gov search. The totals on this page count each donor's own outbound giving once; they do not attempt to trace money a second time after it passes through a PAC.

Conclusion & questions readers should ask

This page documents the energy sector's giving separately — 10 companies and PACs (PacifiCorp, Union Pacific Railroad, EnergySolutions, Torus, Big West Oil, Chevron, rPlus Energies, Edison Electric Institute, Dominion Energy PAC, Utah Petroleum Association PAC), heavily concentrated on Gov. Cox and Utah's legislative leadership committees. Cox is the top documented recipient across nearly every entity on this page. Several donors here (PacifiCorp, EnergySolutions, Big West Oil) show an escalating giving pattern in years leading up to and following Cox's "Operation Gigawatt" nuclear/energy initiative. Big West Oil's giving goes mainly to Cox and the leadership committees, with $250-$500 checks to individual legislators in 2021-2023. Union Pacific Railroad — a completely different industry (rail, not energy) — is included here because its giving pattern overlaps so heavily with PacifiCorp's own donor-legislator list that the two are best read side by side.

Questions worth asking, based on what's documented here:

  • What is the relationship, if any, between the timing of these companies' larger checks and specific energy-policy legislation or state initiatives during the same period?
  • Why does giving from this sector concentrate so heavily on the Governor and legislative leadership PACs rather than spreading across individual legislators, compared to other industries?
  • Dominion Energy PAC filed its dissolution on 9/27/2024, yet a $10,000 check to Spencer Cox (8/19/2025) and $6,000 to UHREC (1/8/2026) appear under its name in other filers' records. Which entity actually wrote those checks?
  • Chevron funds the Utah Petroleum Association PAC, and a Chevron executive is that PAC's vice chairman. How much of the PAC's giving is Chevron money passed through?
  • A recurring group of legislators (Ronald Winterton, Joel Ferry, Evan Vickers, Darlene Pierucci, Todd Weiler, Jim Dunnigan, Logan Wilde, and others) take checks from both PacifiCorp and Union Pacific in the same years — what, if anything, connects the two industries' interest in the same legislators?

These are open questions the public record raises, not conclusions this page draws. Readers should look at the primary sources linked throughout and decide for themselves what the documented facts mean.
