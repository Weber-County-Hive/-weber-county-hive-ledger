The Weber County Hive · Money-Flow Ledger

Follow the Money

A tip about one housing nonprofit led to a Republican political committee that moved $730,000 through consultants, a self-dealing legislative treasurer, and a Washington DC Super PAC attorney's name.

← Back to the Ledger

A public tweet started this: a claim that the Utah Legislature funded a nonprofit whose registered agent is also the Governor's own campaign manager. Every part of that claim checks out against primary sources — and following the money past it leads through both chambers of the Utah GOP's own campaign committees, a $25,000 personal payment to a legislative leadership treasurer, and a Washington DC attorney whose name keeps reappearing across Utah's political money network. Here's the trail, one confirmed step at a time.

For Beginners

Wait — what actually happened here?

Think of it like this. Your state government has a jar of tax money set aside to help people afford housing. Instead of giving that money straight to a housing project, lawmakers gave $2 million of it to a brand-new group — one that didn't exist a few months earlier — to run TV ads about housing.

1

The people running that new group are the same people who run the Governor's campaigns and consult for the Attorney General.

2

That group paid $75,000 to one of those same people's own private company — for the ads.

3

Meanwhile, a separate committee received $360,000 from the Utah House and Senate Republicans' own campaign funds and an out-of-state $100,000 individual gift — then paid that same private company again, paid a national polling firm and a national PR firm, and sent $25,000 directly to the treasurer who controls both House and Senate GOP PAC money.

4

That committee was set up by a Washington DC lawyer who has built five other similar Utah groups — all connected to the same small circle of donors and officials.

None of this is proven to be illegal. But it shows how a small circle of people can move public and political money back and forth to each other, through groups the public can't easily see into. That's what "follow the money" means here — and below is the paper trail, sourced document by document.

Tier 1 — The tip, verified

What Utah Workforce Housing Advocacy actually got

UWHA — a nonprofit incorporated just weeks before the 2024 legislative session — received $1 million in direct, no-bid legislative appropriations in 2024, then another $1 million in FY25-26. No competing bidder. Matt Lusty, founder of the political consulting firm Election Hive and on record as "the campaign spokesperson" for Gov. Spencer Cox, is UWHA's registered agent and has been described as its executive director. Steve Waldrip — Cox's own Senior Advisor for Housing Strategy — sits on UWHA's board while holding that state role at the same time.

Source: Utah Legislature appropriations records; Fox 13 (7/29/2025); UWHA's own board listing
Tier 2 — Where the money actually went

UWHA's own financial reports

UWHA paid at least $75,000 to Honey Communications — Matt Lusty's second firm — for its "Demand More Supply" ad campaign. When UWHA's own leadership was asked to name a single house built as a result of the campaign, the answer was a pause and "that's a great question."

Source: UWHA financial disclosures; Fox 13 (7/29/2025)

A second, separate payment to the same firm

A completely different filer, "Utahns for Balanced Government," paid Honey Communications $8,950 in October 2024 — a single payment, but one piece of a much larger operation this filer was running that same year.

Source: disclosures.utah.gov, DEX filing data
Tier 3 — The full ledger of Utahns for Balanced Government

A committee registered where PAC searches don't look

Utahns for Balanced Government is registered as a Political Issues Committee (PIC) — a different category than a PAC under Utah law, and one that doesn't appear in a standard PAC-only search or directory browse. It files real reports under this category. Its full 2023–2026 ledger, pulled directly from the state's DEX filing system, shows $360,000 in contributions and $370,350 in expenditures.

Source: disclosures.utah.gov DEX filing exports, Utahns for Balanced Government

The treasurer who controls the money coming in also got money going out

Kyle Palmer is Treasurer of UHREC — the House GOP PAC that gave this committee $100,000 — and holds the same treasurer role at four other Utah PACs simultaneously. On 4/3/2025, this same committee paid $25,000 directly to Irish Elk LLC, Palmer's own consulting company. That's not money flowing through Palmer's PAC infrastructure — it's a direct personal payment to him from the committee his own PAC helped fund.

Source: disclosures.utah.gov DEX filing exports; Utah PAC officer registrations

Two large donors got their money back, same day

On 10/17/2024, the committee returned $100,000 in full to the Utah Bankers Association and $10,000 in full to Spring Canyon Capital LLC — both labeled "Return of a Contribution." The filing doesn't say why. It could reflect a donor's own request, a compliance concern, or a change of plans — the record doesn't specify, and neither should any characterization of it until it does.

Source: disclosures.utah.gov DEX filing exports

The committee's own leaders are also its paid vendors

The committee's official officer filing lists Executive Director Marty Carpenter's email at the domain "24nine.com" — and the very first expenditure in the ledger is $10,000 paid to 24NINE on 8/28/2023, labeled "Constituent Services." Separately, the filing lists Mike McCauley as the committee's Treasurer — and "McCauley & Associates," the recurring small-dollar vendor throughout the ledger, has drawn roughly $7,000 across seven payments since 2024. Treasurers charging their own committees compliance fees isn't unusual on its own, but between this, the Executive Director's own firm, and the direct payment to Kyle Palmer above, the pattern is the same three times over: money moving from the committee back to the people who run it.

Source: disclosures.utah.gov PIC officer filing; DEX filing exports
Tier 4 — Who actually built it

A Washington DC Super PAC attorney's name, again

The person who filed Utahns for Balanced Government's paperwork in 2018 was Matthew T. Sanderson of Caplin & Drysdale — a national Super PAC attorney who is also the incorporator of five other Utah political entities: All In For Utah Foundation, All In For Utah PAC, the Utah Doers Network, the Pioneer Trail Foundation, and a federal Defending Utah Values Super PAC. The committee's registered agent is Marty Carpenter — former campaign manager to Gov. Gary Herbert — alongside directors Brad Taylor and Tyler Carpenter. Carpenter originally formed the group in 2018 to oppose a ballot measure, funded almost entirely by Herbert's own PAC; it sat dormant for roughly six years before this 2024 reactivation.

Source: Utah Division of Corporations, Articles of Incorporation; Salt Lake Tribune (10/30/2018)

Closing the loop

Derek Brown — the sitting Attorney General — was also a UWHA director, starting the same month he opened his AG campaign. Gov. Cox's own campaign is Election Hive's single largest client, paying it more than $244,000 across two single-day payments. A tip about one nonprofit and three names connects outward into the same donor and PAC network that funds the Governor, the Attorney General, and House and Senate leadership all at once.

Source: Utah campaign finance disclosures; UWHA board records

A note on how to read this

  • Being registered as a Political Issues Committee is itself lawful — it's a real, filed category under Utah law, not a loophole. What's newsworthy is how much money moved through it and to whom, not the registration type itself.
  • Sanderson is a national attorney with many clients. His role as incorporator across six Utah entities is a real, documented pattern — not evidence that he personally directs any of these organizations.
  • The reasons behind the two full-refund transactions (Utah Bankers Association, Spring Canyon Capital) are not stated in the public filing. Don't assume a motive the record doesn't show.
  • The $25,000 payment to Irish Elk LLC is a documented transaction between the committee and Kyle Palmer's company — it is not, on its own, evidence of what the payment was for beyond the "Other" purpose code on the filing.
