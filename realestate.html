Who these people are

The same handful of names run nearly every real-estate-industry PAC on this page. Here's each one, broken out.

Mike Ostermiller
CEO, Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors — also a MIDA board member since 2008
Show documented connections
  • Paid $246,720 total by NWAOR (2023 Form 990) — separately paid $5,000 directly by NWAOR's own PAC in 2023, on top of his salary
  • Registered Utah lobbyist since 2012 for NWAOR, Utah Association of Realtors, and Utah Property Rights Coalition — paid by his own association as staff, then paid again by that same association as its hired lobbyist
  • MIDA board seat is a direct Governor appointment under the "interested in military efforts" category — not an unexplained loophole, a deliberate choice of who fills that discretionary seat
  • Co-director with Nilson Homes CEO Jed Nilson of two nonprofits (The Healing Collective Utah Inc., Nilson Foundation Inc.), independent of any PAC money on this page
Full profile, including the KKOS lobbying-partner tension with his own sworn disclosure form, is in the site's Unelected Power series.
Chris Kyler
CEO, Utah Association of Realtors — Ostermiller's lobbying partner through KKOS Lawyers
Show documented connections
  • $908,507 total compensation, 2024 (UAR's own Form 990) — the largest single-person compensation figure found anywhere in this investigation
  • Registers jointly with Ostermiller through KKOS for the same 90+ clients since 2012, with matching begin/end dates — Blue Castle Holdings (nuclear), Utah Inland Port Authority, Intermountain Power Agency, SITLA
  • Both named co-defendants (along with NWAOR) in a 2013 lawsuit, dismissed on statute-of-limitations grounds before reaching the merits — a documented direct link between the two men
Mike Schultz
Speaker of the Utah House — also President of Northern Wasatch HBA PAC (home builders, not the Realtors' PAC)
Show documented connections
  • Listed as Primary Officer (President/Home Builder) alongside CEO Paul Ray on Northern Wasatch HBA PAC's own filing — has decision-making authority over the PAC, per that filing
  • NWHBA sits structurally outside the rest of his PAC network — its Treasurer is Rick Draper, a banker, not Kyle Palmer (who treasures HSLPAC, UHREC, NUPAC, and UCL PAC), and it files from a different address than those four
  • Its 2026 Primary Report shows 12 itemized contributions, $50-$100 each, $2,650 total raised and zero expenditures — these are member businesses paying standard NWHBA membership dues rather than each independently choosing to give, though that pooled dues money still funds a PAC he directs
  • Separately received $2,000 from Utah Association of Realtors' own personal-account giving in 2026 (two checks) — a direct RPAC-adjacent donor relationship distinct from the HBA PAC role
  • His own official bio lists his profession as "Agriculture," not real estate or homebuilding, despite this PAC role and his business background
Mark Shepherd
NWAOR's own President — sitting Mayor of Clearfield, also a MIDA board member
Show documented connections

  • His own sworn 2025 financial disclosure (a separate filing from campaign finance reports) shows $5,000+ in consulting income from Lotus Company, an Ogden developer that repeatedly receives state tax-exempt bond allocations — a direct personal financial tie worth its own look

Conclusion & questions readers should ask

This page documents the real estate industry's giving separately — five PACs (RPAC, Salt Lake Board of Realtors, NWAOR, National Association of REALTORS Fund, Northern Wasatch HBA PAC) plus a "Who these people are" section covering seven individuals whose roles connect repeatedly across this network: the same two men (Mike Ostermiller, Chris Kyler) run the two largest local Realtor associations and lobby jointly through the same firm; several officials (Mark Shepherd, Gage Froerer, J. Stuart Adams) hold both a real-estate-industry leadership role and a public office or board seat at the same time; and one PAC's own president (Shepherd) and CEO (Ostermiller) are both paid directly by the association they lead. Salt Lake Board of Realtors is a notable outlier — its recipient list has zero confirmed current-or-former officeholders, entirely vendors and consultants.

Questions worth asking, based on what's documented here:

  • When the same two people run both of the state's two largest local Realtor associations and lobby jointly for the same 90+ clients, what does that concentration mean for how the industry's political and legislative interests get represented?
  • When a trade association's own elected president or CEO is paid directly by that same association's PAC, what governs whether that reflects fair compensation versus a closed financial loop?
  • What obligations, if any, exist for an official who sits on a state land-use authority board (like MIDA) while also holding a leadership role in an industry association that lobbies that same authority?
  • Why does one of the largest PACs on this page (Salt Lake Board of Realtors) show no documented giving to any current or former officeholder at all — is that a genuine difference in strategy, or a gap in what's been researched so far?
  • What is the actual nature of the consulting relationship between a sitting mayor (Shepherd) and a developer (Lotus Company) that repeatedly receives state tax-exempt bond allocations?

These are open questions the public record raises, not conclusions this page draws. Readers should look at the primary sources linked throughout and decide for themselves what the documented facts mean.
