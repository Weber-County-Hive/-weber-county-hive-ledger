Follow the Money · Case 03

The Roybal Chain: How One PAC Funds a County Commissioner, a Realtors' Association, and the Man Who Registered It at His Own House

A single political action committee — registered at the home address of the man who runs an economic development alliance — sits at the center of money flowing from developers, a state Senate President's own PAC, and a real estate trade association, into the campaign account of the Weber County Commissioner who then voted to advance the projects those same interests wanted built. And the county's own economic development director is, at the same time, a paid lobbyist for the water district that governs the project's own infrastructure.

Who Chris Roybal is, and where his PAC lives

Chris Roybal is President of the Northern Utah Economic Alliance (NUEA), a joint Davis-Weber County economic development organization. NUEA is one of the named marketing partners on the West Weber Inland Port project, alongside the Utah Inland Port Authority (UIPA) itself and the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity. Roybal is listed by name as a contact on UIPA's own 2025-2026 West Weber Annual Report.

The address: Utah Forward PAC, LLC's official registered address on file with the state is Chris Roybal's own home address. He is not only a donor to this PAC and one of its managers — the PAC's legal existence is anchored at his residence.

Weber County Commissioner Gage Froerer serves as Weber County's Co-Chair on NUEA's Advisory Board — a role Froerer's own campaign has not hidden, but one that places him, structurally, inside the same organization Roybal runs and that markets the port project he later votes to advance.

How Nilson's project grew through the approval process

2024A new state law, HB527, makes $300 million available in low-interest developer loans from the Transportation Investment Fund for owner-occupied affordable housing. Nilson's representative tells county commissioners the company worked directly with Steve Waldrip, Governor Cox's senior housing adviser, on the resulting plan.
Jul 2024The Weber County Commission approves an amendment adding 275 "attainable" homes, growing the project to 1,000 total units. Sharon Bolos, one of the three commissioners voting, publicly champions the expansion: "Attainability is what this is all about."
Oct 2024Governor Cox personally attends the groundbreaking for the first 275 starter homes, framing it as progress toward his statewide "35,000 starter homes" goal.

Nilson Homes now operates four active communities in Weber County: Orchards at JDC Ranch and The Grove at JDC Ranch (both Plain City), Riverbend Farms (Ogden), and Stagecoach Estates (Taylor).

The sewer money and a state-level abstention

The infrastructure underneath all of this — a lift station, a 14-inch force main, a 30-inch gravity line to Central Weber Sewer — was partly financed with a $5 million state ARPA grant. Two state board members wouldn't vote on it.

Utah Water Quality Board minutes from January 25, 2023 confirm the Board authorized $5 million in ARPA funding for this exact infrastructure. On that vote, board members Michela Harris and Joseph Havasi abstained, citing a conflict of interest — the minutes themselves don't say what the conflict was.

By March 3, 2026, the Weber County Commission's Resolution 10-2026 — the same resolution behind the $25.9 million PCC PID bond that Gibson's former property now sits inside — had PCC PID No. 1 carrying the entire bond burden alone through assessment bonds. Russell described this on the record as "part of the match money required" for the sewer project, consistent with satisfying the state grant's own matching-funds condition.

Structural coordination between Adams and Froerer runs through the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) board — a separate state authority. Froerer's MIDA seat isn't tied to him personally — it's a gubernatorial appointment drawn from whichever three people sit on the Weber County Commission, a seat he's held since 2019.
Froerer's own disclosure form has a place for this, and leaves it blank — twice. His sworn conflict-of-interest form asks him to list every board and paid-leadership position he holds. Both years, he leaves off Little Mountain Service Area and MIDA — two board seats independently confirmed active during the same filing periods.

The relationship that predates all of it: Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors

Separate from the Utah Forward PAC chain, Froerer has a documented, sustained giving relationship with the Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors (NWAR) that runs longer and deeper than any single PAC transaction — and the relationship itself runs back a century.

The Froerer family ran NWAOR for four generations. Fred Froerer Sr. served as its president in 1922, 1926, 1932, 1933, 1937, and 1938. Fred Froerer Jr. served in 1959. Fredrick "Buck" Froerer III in 1981. Zane Froerer in 1988. Gage Froerer himself in 1990. NWAOR is not a registered PAC — its contributions to Froerer are direct and in-kind giving from the association itself.

NWAOR's own broader giving record shows $29,000 in direct contributions to Froerer's campaign in total, plus $5,050 in-kind to Sharon Bolos — the larger figure likely reflects additional years or checks beyond these four; reconciling the complete itemized record against the $29,000 total is still open.

NWAR's CEO throughout this entire period is Mike Ostermiller — a sitting Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA) board member since 2008, the same authority Froerer's own seat sits on. Ostermiller was also a registered UIPA lobbyist from January to December 2022 — meaning NWAR's own March 2022 donation to Froerer landed the same year its CEO was actively lobbying the authority that oversees West Weber.

Ostermiller's own footprint reaches well beyond NWAR and MIDA: he's a partner at a government-affairs law firm (Kyler, Kohler, Ostermiller & Sorenson), holds six simultaneous MIDA-family board seats, sits on the Utah Land Use Institute, the Utah Private Property Ombudsmen Board, the Davis Economic Advisory Council, the Layton City Board of Adjustments, and the Housing Affordability Commission, and is registered to lobby for the Utah Association of Realtors and the Utah Property Rights Coalition. Utah reporting has also tied him to the 2020 Hideout annexation bill — the controversial legislation that let a small town annex distant land near Park City for large-scale development without county consent, later struck down after statewide backlash.

Ostermiller and Jed Nilson — CEO of Nilson Homes, the $20,000 Utah Forward PAC donor documented earlier in this piece — are co-directors of an Ogden nonprofit, The Healing Collective Utah Inc., incorporated August 2025. The tie runs deeper than that: Utah's corporate registry shows Jed Nilson as President of a second entity, the Nilson Foundation Inc., formed July 2026 — with Ostermiller personally serving as its Incorporator, Registered Agent, and Treasurer. Ostermiller didn't just join Nilson's foundation; he filed the paperwork that created it.

R&O Construction shows the same repeat-donor pattern in miniature: $1,000 directly to Froerer in 2018, then $5,000 to Utah Forward PAC in 2022 — two paths to the same recipient, four years apart.

A separate thread: Froerer's own family business and the Weber Housing Authority

This one isn't about West Weber or Utah Forward PAC — it's a different conflict entirely, involving a public housing authority, Froerer's own family real estate business, and a third board seat missing from his own disclosure form.

$19,623
Weber Housing Authority payments to Froerer family companies, 2023–2025
2
Froerers serving as Housing Authority trustees — Gage and his wife, Gloria

Gage Froerer is a trustee of the Weber Housing Authority. So is his wife, Gloria, who also directs its affiliated nonprofit, Friends of the Weber Housing Authority. Weber County's own warrant reports, reviewed month by month from January 2023 through December 2025, show $19,623 in payments from the Housing Authority to two Froerer family companies: RESCOM INC and Froerer Real Estate and Property Management.

Weber County itself processes all of the Housing Authority's administrative and financial business, under an interlocal agreement between the two. That means the same county government Froerer sits on as Commissioner handles the payments a housing authority — where he and his wife both serve as trustees — makes to his own family's companies.

Neither of Froerer's own sworn 2025 or 2026 disclosure forms lists the Weber Housing Authority under the section asking for board and paid-leadership positions. Both years list only two water and sewer districts. It's the third confirmed board seat missing from that same form — alongside the Military Installation Development Authority and Little Mountain Service Area seats already documented in this piece.

Froerer's real estate business runs deep in Weber County: Froerer Real Estate and Property Management LLC has operated since 1914, and the family has led the Northern Wasatch Association of Realtors across four generations, documented earlier in this piece. Two of Froerer's sons, Casey and Ryan, run the family's ResCom Real Estate Management business today.

Why this matters

None of the individual transactions here are illegal. Utah's disclosure laws exist precisely so this kind of giving is visible, checkable, and public. What this chain shows is not a secret — it's a pattern that only becomes visible when the pieces are put next to each other: a PAC registered at an economic-development official's own home, funded partly by a state Senate President's own leadership PAC, giving annual "Political Support" payments to a county commissioner who sits on the advisory board of the organization marketing the very project he later votes to advance — layered on top of a real estate trade association's own decade-long giving relationship with the same commissioner, and a public housing authority paying that same commissioner's own family business.

Every figure above is checkable at disclosures.utah.gov and Utah's Division of Corporations business search.
