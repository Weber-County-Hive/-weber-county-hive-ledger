CASE 05

The Payment That Disappeared

A $62,500 check to a company managed by MIDA's own director appears in one official report on a taxpayer-funded housing nonprofit and is absent from the next — and the state's own filings show that nonprofit's registered leadership doesn't match the board it shows the public.

Who's who

A handful of names — and one company — come up throughout this story. Tap or click a card to flip it — here's who they are and what they've done in Utah politics before this piece even starts.

In January 2025, Utah Workforce Housing Advocacy (UWHA) — a nonprofit built around a $2 million legislative appropriation — filed an interim spending report with the state. It showed two checks, totaling $62,500, paid to a company called BizDevOps Consulting, LLC. Five months later, UWHA's year-end final report to the same state office covered the same money, the same period, the same account. The two BizDevOps checks were gone.

Tracing the company those checks went to leads to a registered agent that is, in turn, managed by Daniel Hemmert — now the Executive Director of the Military Installation Development Authority (MIDA), a state land-use authority. The entity chain behind that payment was founded in 2014, a decade before UWHA existed, using Hemmert's own long-standing business vehicle.

Separately, UWHA's own filing with the Utah Division of Corporations shows a set of officers and directors that largely doesn't match the "board" the nonprofit lists on its own website.

Two reports, one missing vendor

UWHA reports its spending from the state appropriation to the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity twice a cycle: a mid-campaign interim report and a year-end final report. Both are supposed to cover the same money. They don't agree.

The two BizDevOps checks were gone.

Interim report

Submitted January 24, 2025 — covers 7/1/24 through 1/13/25
  • Check #4007 — $50,000 to BizDevOps Consulting, LLC (10/25/24), labeled "Consulting"
  • Check #4013 — $12,500 to BizDevOps Consulting, LLC (11/29/24)

Final report

Submitted June 26, 2025 — covers 7/1/24 through 6/26/25
  • No check numbered 4007
  • No check numbered 4013
  • No mention of BizDevOps Consulting anywhere in the itemized budget
Both checks, and the vendor, are simply absent.
The arithmetic checks out. Add up every check listed in the interim report, including the two BizDevOps payments, and the total comes to $928,187.98. Subtract the $62,500 BizDevOps paid, and the result is $865,687.98 — the exact total UWHA's own final report gives for its spending. The missing checks account for the entire gap between the two totals.

Where the $62,500 went

BizDevOps Consulting, LLC was formed on February 27, 2024 — after the Legislature had already approved UWHA's first $1 million. Its own filing with the state lists a single principal: not a person, but another company.

BizDevOps Consulting, LLC Formed 2/27/2024. Registered agent and sole Manager: RHRD, LLC.
↓ registered agent / sole manager
RHRD, LLC Formed 3/31/2014. Managers: Daniel Hemmert and Ryan Fish. Registered agent and Member: FISHCO, LLC.
↑ founded by, using his own entity as registered agent
Daniel Hemmert Organizer of RHRD, LLC in 2014, using HEMMCO, LLC — his own company — as its original registered agent and Manager. Now Executive Director of MIDA.

RHRD's 2014 founding certificate — stamped received by the Utah Division of Corporations on March 31 of that year — lists Daniel Hemmert as the organizer and HEMMCO, LLC as both the registered agent and the original Manager. HEMMCO is Hemmert's own entity, independently documented elsewhere in Utah campaign-finance filings as his registered agent and sole Manager. The second founding Manager, FISHCO, LLC, belongs to Ryan Fish, who remains a Manager today alongside Hemmert, per RHRD's most recent amendment, filed with the state on June 3, 2026.

RHRD LLC is Hemmert's own decade-plus business vehicle, not a company formed for this one transaction. It sits, alone, as the only registered principal of the company that received $62,500 from UWHA — a payment that appeared in the interim report and did not appear in the final one.

Two different rosters at UWHA

UWHA's own website lists a six-person board and a six-person "Policy Board" — twelve names, mostly real estate developers and lenders. That's a different set of people than the state's own corporate filing lists as UWHA's Officer and Directors.

Only Waldrip appears on both lists. Matt Lusty — the Election Hive founder who's publicly described elsewhere as UWHA's executive director — isn't on the public board at all, because his actual legal role with the state is Officer, a different category. Heather Groom, a former deputy campaign manager to Gov. Gary Herbert, has a formal state-registered director role that appears nowhere in UWHA's own public materials. And Derek Brown, now Utah's Attorney General, is still listed as a Director in the state's own filing — no resignation is reflected in that record, even though he's absent from the website UWHA shows the public.

Whether UWHA's public board has any actual legal authority over the organization, or functions as an advisory layer separate from the four people the state recognizes as running it, is not answered by either filing on its own.

What isn't established here

This piece documents two things directly, from primary records: the disappearance of the BizDevOps payment between two official reports, and the mismatch between UWHA's public board and its state-registered Officer/Directors. It does not establish why either happened — no explanation for the omission, and no confirmation of what authority, if any, UWHA's public-facing board holds. Both are open questions.
