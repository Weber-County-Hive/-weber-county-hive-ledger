Gambling Money in Utah, Made Simple

Posted Nov. 3, 2026 · Updated Nov. 3, 2026

This is the easy version. Want every donation, the map, the timeline and all the sources?See the full version →

The short story

Gambling is against the law in Utah. But gambling companies still gave $115,800 to Utah political campaigns from 2018 to 2026.

Most of it, $83,000, went to Attorney General Derek Brown. His office is now suing and charging Kalshi, a betting app.

The records show who gave and how much. They don't show why.

Who got the money

Derek Brown (AG)
$83,000
21 other candidates
$22,800
Sean Reyes (AG, 2018 and 2023)
$7,500

$2,500
Any Utah PAC

Brown got more than every other Utah candidate in nine years put together.

Did he sue the companies that paid him?

No. The company Brown's office sued and charged, Kalshi, gave nothing to any Utah campaign.

The companies that did give to Brown have not been sued or charged by Brown's office, as far as we found.

The open question is whether they're being treated the same as Kalshi.

The biggest gift

Las Vegas Sands, a big casino company, gave Brown $50,000 on April 21, 2026. That was two months after Brown wrote that prediction markets are gambling. It gave Utah money only one other time: $5,000 to the attorney general before him, Sean Reyes, in 2018.

Las Vegas Sands has spent years fighting online gambling, which competes with its casinos. Brown's office is fighting an online betting app. So the gift may line up with what Brown is already doing. No record says why it was given.

The Utah way

  • Gambling has been against Utah's constitution since statehood. No casinos, no lottery, no Powerball.
  • Utah voters turned down horse-race betting in 1992.
  • About half of Utahns are Latter-day Saints. On Oct. 3, 2026, Church leader D. Todd Christofferson said "gambling is morally wrong."
  • Gov. Cox, as quoted by AP, said betting apps put "a casino in the pocket of every single American."

That's the backdrop for the $115,800 on this page.

Why do gambling companies give in a state that bans gambling?

No record shows anyone's reasons. But here is what each side says in public:

  • The companies say they aren't gambling. The sweepstakes company says no one has to pay to play. The pick'em app says winning takes skill. Kalshi says its bets are federal financial contracts.
  • The federal agency over these markets (the CFTC) sides with Kalshi and sued Utah over it.
  • Some lawmakers worried the ban could go too far, for example into fantasy sports.
  • The gifts came after the vote. 17 of the 21 lawmakers who got sweepstakes money voted for the 2026 ban on prop bets, months earlier.

Why the companies give, and what they expect, is still unknown. You can ask them; the questions are on the full version.

Is this allowed?

Yes. Utah has no limit on how much a person or company can give to a state candidate. We found no Utah rule that keeps an attorney general off a case because of a donation.

Can I get in trouble for using these apps?

Utah law says taking part in gambling, including online, is a class B misdemeanor. As far as we found, only Kalshi and its two founders have been charged, not users.

Is Brown on today's ballot?

No. He was elected in 2024. His next election is in 2028. But 17 of the 21 lawmakers who got money from the sweepstakes company VGW are on today's ballot. Their names are on the full version.

What we know, and what we don't

We know

  • Who gave, how much, and when (state records)
  • Kalshi gave nothing to any Utah campaign
  • Kalshi's Utah lobbyist once worked at the same firm as Brown
  • Brown's office sued Kalshi Oct. 20 and filed charges Oct. 28, 2026
  • The federal agency over these markets sued Utah on Oct. 27, 2026

We don't know

  • Why any company gave
  • If any donor asked Brown for anything
  • Why other betting apps that gave to Brown haven't been charged
  • If Brown will keep the money

Words to know

Prediction market
An app where people bet on what will happen, like who wins a game or an election. Kalshi is one. It says its bets are legal under federal rules. Utah says they're gambling.
Sweepstakes casino
An online site with casino-style games that uses "coins" instead of cash bets, so it says it isn't gambling. VGW and Arb Gaming run these.
Fantasy "pick'em" app
An app where you pick how well players will do. PrizePicks and Underdog are examples.
Lobbyist
A person paid to talk to lawmakers and officials for a company or group. They must register with the state.
PAC
Political action committee: a group that collects money and gives it to campaigns.

Questions you can ask

  1. Ask the AG's office (attorneygeneral.utah.gov): Will you keep or give back the gambling money?
  2. Ask the AG's office: Are the other companies that gave to your campaign part of your "larger investigation"?
  3. Ask your legislators (le.utah.gov): Did you get money from VGW, and will you vote on gambling bills in 2027?
  4. Ask your legislators: Does Utah law clearly cover sweepstakes sites, pick'em apps and prediction markets?

All 15 questions are on the full version.
