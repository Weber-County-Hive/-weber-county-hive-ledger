Who Gets Paid to Sell You a Candidate

The short, simple version. No tables.

Posted Nov. 17, 2026 · Updated Nov. 17, 2026

Want every number and every source?Read the full case file →

The big idea

Campaigns spend most of their money on selling the candidate: billboards, mail, signs, ads and the people who plan them. Some of the companies that sell those things also give money to candidates. Both kinds of money have to be reported to the state. We looked them up.

One billboard company, two kinds of money

$183,276Reagan Outdoor Advertising gave to Utah campaigns in 2026
$93,725.73Campaigns paid Reagan in 2026, mostly for ads

These are two different things. One is gifts. The other is payments for billboards. Don't add them together.

The biggest gift was $50,000 to Gov. Spencer Cox on Sept. 14, 2026. Most lawmakers got $700 to $1,500.

Eight campaigns were on both lists

Reagan gave money to eight campaigns, and those same eight paid Reagan for ads. (A ninth paid someone listed only as "Reagan," so we can't confirm it yet.) That shows they were both donor and customer. It does not show a special deal or anything wrong. The state's search doesn't say if a gift was cash or free billboard space, so we can't tell yet.

The rules

  • TV and radio: if a station sells time to one candidate, it must give the others in that race the same chance. That's federal law.
  • Billboards don't have that rule in Utah. Minnesota has one. Utah does not, as far as we found.
  • In 2026, Utah passed a law (H.B. 33). Starting Jan. 1, 2027, the "paid for by" line must be easy to read on billboards, and more signs must have one. The first version also said the line had to be readable on ordinary signs, but that part was dropped.

The firms that run campaigns

  • Election Hive runs campaigns. Its manager, Matthew Lusty, also runs Honey Communications from the same office.
  • Roost makes campaign ads. Sen. Stuart Adams's PAC paid it $49,861.06 in 2026.
  • Gathering Inc. collects signatures to get candidates on the ballot. In 2025, some people who had gathered for it were charged with forging signatures. Most fake signatures were caught. Sen. Adams's campaign paid it $76,682 in 2026.
  • The same few names show up again and again: Casey Hill and Spencer Stokes are listed with more than one of these firms.

Mail that shows up at your house

A political mailer must say who paid for it. That rule is about who paid, not whether the mailer is true.

  • 2024: A PAC called Preserving Utah Values sent attack mailers and texts about Democratic lawmakers while reporting $0. A later report showed House Speaker Mike Schultz gave it $120,000.
  • June 2026: Ads from a group called Utahns for Safer Communities ran while its reports for 2026 showed $0. Its 2022 reports list more than $125,000 from Mike Schultz.
  • June 2026: A PAC started by a Democratic consultant sent "Republicans Against" mailers in a Box Elder County Republican primary.
  • 2026: One PAC that filed its report late was fined, so the rules do get enforced sometimes.
  • October 2026: A mailer about Sen. Brady Brammer only said "10,000 Integrity Trackers," and we found no group by that name on file.

Our grade: D

We use the same five-part grade on everything we cover. The rules make campaigns report their money and name who paid for ads, so the basics are there. But people can't see prices, can't easily add up the totals, and sometimes can't tell who is behind an attack until after they vote. The Legislature's record is mixed: some changes help (more signs must name who paid, starting in 2027), and some fall short.

What you can ask

  • Billboard companies: Do all candidates get the same prices?
  • Your lawmakers (le.utah.gov): Should billboards have to treat all candidates the same, like TV does?
  • The state elections office (disclosures.utah.gov): Can the website add up totals for us?

Look it up yourself

Go to disclosures.utah.gov, click Advanced Search, pick Contributions or Expenditures, type a company name and click Find. Try more than one spelling.

Fair to all: Campaigns can hire anyone, and companies can give. These records show who gave and who got paid. They don't show why.
