Who these donors are

Eight tech-sector donors — three companies, a trade group, and four PACs — found largely through keyword searches that also caught dozens of unrelated companies sharing "tech" in their name. Officers are listed as named on each PAC's own state registration.

Lumen Technologies Service Group
National telecom company (formerly CenturyLink), filed from Monroe, LA. Gives every year since 2021 to both parties' leadership committees, often in the same months. Complete 2018-2026: $125,040.
Micron Technologyincl. predecessor IM Flash Technologies
Major national semiconductor company with a large Utah/Lehi manufacturing presence. Gives blanket, systematic small checks to nearly every sitting legislator each cycle — the same structural pattern already documented for Utah Petroleum Association PAC. Its 2018 giving is filed mostly as IM Flash Technologies, the Intel/Micron joint venture Micron fully acquired in 2019. Its Utah giving stopped after a single $300 check in 2021.
Dell Technologies Inc PAC
National computer-hardware company's own PAC, filed from Washington, DC. Registered in Utah in 2024; its only Utah gift is $10,000 to Spencer Cox that year.
Utah Tech LeadsUtah Tech Leads Association / Utah Tech Leads PAC
A Utah tech-industry PAC, filed under three names (Utah Tech Leads, Utah Tech Leads Association, Utah Tech Leads PAC), active only in 2022 and 2024. It received Slopes PAC's entire $71,440 balance and gave broadly across both parties. Its officers haven't been pulled yet.
Utah Tech PACSilicon Slopes Commons PAC
The official PAC of Silicon Slopes Commons, Utah's best-known tech-industry nonprofit hub. President & CEO Clint Betts is Primary Officer; Director of Operations Elizabeth Converse is CFO/Treasurer. Its state registration lists Silicon Slopes Commons as the organization it represents. The state shows "No Activity" today; its own exports show $70,139.09 spent in 2017-2018, including $50,000 to its lobbyist.
Slopes PAC
Created 6/8/2021. Per its Statement of Organization: Sunny Washington (CEO, Primary Officer) and Elizabeth Converse (Executive Director, CFO/Treasurer — the same treasurer as Utah Tech PAC). Received $55,000 from Silicon Slopes Commons; its last report (through 4/13/2022) shows $71,440 on hand.
Silicon Slopes Commons
The Lehi tech-industry nonprofit. Its only political gift on record is $55,000 to Slopes PAC on 12/1/2021, filed from the same Lehi address the PAC registered.
Forge the Future ProjectMeta Platforms' PAC
Per its Statement of Organization: Chair Josh Althouse, Treasurer Khaki Brownlow, officer Brian Baker. Meta is listed as the organization represented. It took in $270,000 and spent $251,446.36 on two candidates on 6/11/2026.
Two names sound alike but are different committees: "Utah Tech Leads" and "Utah Tech PAC" (Silicon Slopes Commons' PAC) are separate filers with different registered addresses. Keep them separate in any write-up.

Conclusion & questions readers should ask

This page documents eight tech-sector donors — Meta's Forge the Future Project, Lumen Technologies, Micron Technology (including IM Flash Technologies), Dell Technologies PAC, Silicon Slopes Commons, Slopes PAC, Utah Tech Leads, and Utah Tech PAC. Micron/IM Flash gave small checks to most sitting legislators in both parties in 2018 and 2020, then stopped. Silicon Slopes Commons' $55,000 passed through Slopes PAC to Utah Tech Leads, so those three totals overlap. This industry generated more excluded "search noise" than any other bucket on this site — dozens of unrelated companies and even individuals caught only because their name contains the string "tech."

Questions worth asking, based on what's documented here:

  • Slopes PAC's last report (through 4/13/2022) shows its $71,440 still on hand, while Utah Tech Leads records receiving it on 4/15/2022. Why does no Slopes PAC report show the transfer?
  • Utah Tech PAC paid its lobbyist $50,000 in 2017, against $13,750 to candidates, parties and PACs. What did the lobbying cover?
  • Meta's PAC spent $251,446.36 on two legislative candidates on one day, 12 days before their primary. What drew a national tech company into those two races?
  • Micron gave to most sitting legislators through 2020 and has reported nothing since 2021. What changed?
  • Is "Utah Tech University" (flagged separately, not one of the five donors on this page) really the public university itself giving to political candidates and party committees, or a data-labeling issue?

These are open questions the public record raises, not conclusions this page draws. Readers should look at the primary sources linked throughout and decide for themselves what the documented facts mean.
